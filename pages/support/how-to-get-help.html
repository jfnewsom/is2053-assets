Discord
Why Discord?

The class Discord is the fastest way to get help. Post your question in your module’s #module-N-help channel and classmates or the instructor will respond, often within minutes. You also get the benefit of seeing how other students have solved similar problems.

How to Ask a Good Question

The better you describe your problem, the faster you’ll get an answer.

  • Describe what you’re trying to do: what is the goal of this code?
  • Share the error message: paste it in a code block so it’s readable
  • Show what you’ve already tried: this helps skip suggestions you’ve already ruled out
  • Be specific: “it doesn’t work” doesn’t help us help you

Send a screenshot, not a phone photo. On Windows press Windows + Shift + S and drag a box around what you want to capture; it lands on your clipboard and pastes straight into Discord with Ctrl + V. On a Mac, Cmd + Shift + 4 does the same thing. Phone pictures work in a pinch, but glare and blur hide exactly the characters we need to read, and the answer is usually sitting in one line of the error message. A clean screenshot means a faster and more accurate fix.

Using Code Blocks

Wrap your code and error messages in triple backticks so Discord formats them correctly. Type ```, paste your code, then ``` again. This makes your question much easier to read.

What to Expect

Tuesday office hours are drop-in, not a lecture. There’s no structured content. What comes up depends on what people bring.

  • Open discussion of the current lab or BookEx assignment
  • General questions that benefit the whole class
  • Announcements and reminders about what’s coming up
  • A space to compare notes with classmates who joined

Nothing is recorded and nothing is posted afterward. Camera optional, and there is no pressure to be on video.

When to Use Tuesday Office Hours

If your question is specific to your code, Discord or Calendly will serve you better. Tuesday office hours are best for questions where the answer helps more than one person: concept reviews, “is this the right approach?” questions, and general assignment orientation.

Book a 1-on-1
When to Book

Need dedicated one-on-one time? Book an appointment through Calendly. You pick the format, in person or online, and the length: 15, 30, 45, or 60 minutes. Great for complex debugging, career questions, or anything that needs focused attention outside the Tuesday session.

Good Reasons to Book
  • You’ve been stuck on the same bug for more than an hour
  • You want to talk through your overall approach before writing more code
  • You have a grade question or concern you’d rather discuss privately
  • You want to chat about Python beyond the coursework, career paths, or next steps

Come Prepared

A booked session goes fast, especially a short one. Have your code open, your error message ready, and a specific question in mind. The more targeted your question, the more we can accomplish in the session.

Email the Instructor
When to Email

Email is best for private matters: grades, accommodations, personal circumstances, or anything you don’t want to discuss publicly. For coding questions, Discord or Tuesday office hours will get you a faster answer.
